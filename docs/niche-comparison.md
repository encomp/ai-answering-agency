## The three bets, in plain terms

1. **PDR — the "empty lane" bet.** Best underserved score, storm-window monopoly potential, but
   seasonal and small on verified math. Win condition: own hail response before anyone else does.
   Counter-seasonal anchor: dealer reconditioning contracts.
2. **Garage door — the "home turf" bet.** The operator's own business: DFW base, verified after-hours
   pain, hail angle, commercial contracts as the recurring B2B core. Smallest market, but the
   operator advantage is real and the economics rest on call capture (their model), not the one-job rule.
3. **House painters — the "volume" bet.** Biggest, safest TAM with verified ad spend and clean
   cash+ — but fought-over. Win condition: quote-speed system ("respond in minutes, quote in hours")
   that no agency sells. Expect agency competition from day one.

## What the coaching call should decide

The framework says lock ONE niche — and the three don't differ on viability, they differ on *which
bet fits the operator's goal*:

- **Least market risk + fastest first clients** → house painters (demand is proven and everywhere;
  the fight is positioning).
- **Most defensible / least agency competition** → PDR (empty lane, storm monopoly potential;
  seasonal risk accepted).
- **Best operator advantage + local gravity** → garage door (home turf, verified pain, commercial
  B2B anchor, hail-alley home base). The natural default for THIS operator.

## Shared proof gaps (all three — same three tests)

1. **Google Maps directory counts (DFW)** — the decisive slice factor per niche (Apify/Skyline tasks).
2. **Veriphone 150-lead samples** — textable % (feeds tier math).
3. **Meta Ads Library run dates** — ad-proof confirmation (human/browser step).
Plus per-niche validation conversations (3–5 owners each) before any build.
