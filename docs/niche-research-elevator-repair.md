## 1. Niche Overview

Elevator and escalator repair and maintenance keeps vertical transportation systems safe, code-compliant, and operational. Scope: passenger elevators, freight elevators, escalators, moving walkways, dumbwaiters, accessibility lifts. Services: emergency repair, preventive maintenance, safety inspections, modernization/retrofit, code-compliance upgrades.

**Market size:**
- US elevator installation & service = **$53.9B in 2026** [VERIFIED: IBISWorld].
- US maintenance services segment = **54.3% of US elevator market** in 2025 [VERIFIED: Market Data Forecast].
- US elevators/escalators market = **$17.1B (2026) → $21.3B by 2031** at 4.47% CAGR [VERIFIED: MarketsandMarkets].
- Global elevator maintenance = **$35.76B (2025) → $68.25B by 2034** at 7.50% CAGR [VERIFIED: Fortune Business Insights].
- Global elevator repair & maintenance service = **$48.62B (2024)** [VERIFIED: OpenPR].
- ~**1 million elevators and escalators** in the US [VERIFIED: NEII — National Elevator Industry Inc].

**Structure:**
- Dominated by "Big 4" OEMs: Otis, Kone, Schindler, Thyssenkrupp — they own new equipment + large service contracts.
- Independent/local service companies: repair shops, inspection firms, modernization contractors, third-party maintenance providers.
- Regulatory layer: state/local safety inspections mandatory; AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction) enforces.
- Long tail of independent operators serving building owners, property managers, and facilities teams.

**Buyers:**
- Building owners / property managers (commercial offices, apartments, hotels, hospitals).
- Facilities management firms (ISS, ABM, Jones Lang LaSalle).
- Real estate developers (new construction → warranty + post-warranty service).
- Government/municipalities (public buildings, transit authorities).
- AHJs / regulatory bodies (inspections, code compliance).

## 4. Demand Triggers & Buyer Psychology

- **Regulatory mandate:** annual safety inspections required in most jurisdictions — creates guaranteed recurring demand for inspection + PM contracts.
- **Aging infrastructure:** US has ~1M elevators/escalators; many installed in 1960s–1980s; modernization wave underway [VERIFIED: MarketsandMarkets, Fortune Business Insights].
- **IoT/predictive maintenance:** smart elevators with sensors create data-driven service opportunities [VERIFIED: Grand View Research, Fortune Business Insights].
- **Emergency downtime:** broken elevator = building partially disabled; high-rise offices, hotels, hospitals cannot function without vertical transport.
- **Seasonality:** low — year-round demand. Inspection cycles create predictable peaks (annual recertification periods).
- **Urgency:** high but NOT emergency-same-day for most calls. Safety entrapment = extreme urgency; stuck elevator = moderate urgency; PM scheduling = low urgency.
- **Trust requirement:** very high — buyer needs certified technicians, code compliance knowledge, and proven safety record. Licensing/certification is mandatory in most states.
- **Price sensitivity:** medium — safety and compliance are non-negotiable; but buyers shop aggressively on PM contract pricing.
